EVERA — Strategic Case Study on a Caffeine-Free Coffee Startup

A field-based strategy project on EVERA, a startup turning upcycled date seeds and chicory root into a caffeine-free coffee alternative — built on a founder interview and supporting market research.

TypeEnd-Term Strategic Management Project
SectorFunctional Beverages
Primary SourceFounder Interview
Frameworks4 Applied
Objective

Apply core strategic management frameworks to a real early-stage startup to evaluate its market opportunity, resource position and competitive strategy — grounded in a founder interview rather than a hypothetical case.

Methodology & Process

Primary research (a structured founder interview) was layered with secondary market data, then analyzed through four complementary strategy frameworks.

01

Primary Research

Conducted a remote, structured interview with the founder to understand the origin story, pain points observed, and the venture's core idea pillars.

02

Secondary Market Research

Gathered supporting data from EVERA's own business presentation and third-party market research on India's coffee and health-beverage market.

03

Value Chain & Cost Modeling

Mapped the five-stage value chain (sourcing, drying, roasting, grinding, packaging) and built a full monthly cost model to derive unit economics and break-even volume.

Four frameworks were then applied to structure the strategic analysis itself:

Porter's Five Forces Buyer Power: High

Assessed industry attractiveness — moderate rivalry and entry threat, low-to-moderate supplier power (date seeds are a low-value byproduct), but high buyer power and high threat of substitutes (decaf coffee, herbal teas, functional beverages).

Resource-Based View (RBV) Internal Resources

Identified the sustainable date-seed raw material source, the novel product concept, the founder's analytical skills, and academic-network mentorship as EVERA's core internal resources.

VRIO Analysis Temporary Advantage

Found EVERA holds temporary sustainable competitive advantage in its upcycled ingredient and early experimentation knowledge — durability depends on strengthening branding and distribution before competitors enter the niche.

Porter's Generic Strategy Focus Differentiation

Classified EVERA's strategy as Focus Differentiation — a narrow, health- and eco-conscious urban segment served with a highly differentiated, premium-yet-accessible product.

Assumptions & Scope
  • Findings rest on a single founder interview as the primary data source — the analysis reflects the founder's account of pain points, resources and strategy rather than independently verified operational data.
  • Market statistics (coffee market size, consumer sentiment, diabetes prevalence) are drawn from third-party market research cited in EVERA's own business presentation, not independently re-verified survey work.
  • The cost model and break-even volume (4,270 units/month) are based on the startup's own projected batch economics at early scale — actual unit costs would shift with real production volume and supplier terms.
  • The VRIO conclusion of "temporary" rather than "sustained" competitive advantage is explicitly conditional — it assumes competitors could replicate the upcycled-ingredient concept once EVERA proves the category, absent stronger brand/distribution moats.
Key Findings

Consumer sentiment: high daily coffee dependency, but a clear desire to cut caffeine and a perceived gap in good alternatives.

India's health burden (diabetics and pre-diabetics) strengthens the case for a functional, health-oriented beverage category.

Unit economics on a 100g pack: ~₹80 cost vs. ~₹174 selling price — a ~54% gross margin at the startup's modeled early-scale volumes.

  • India's coffee powder market is projected to reach ~$1.91B by 2030 at a 7.8% CAGR — a large, growing category rather than a niche experiment.
  • 82% of respondents believe no good caffeine-free alternative currently exists — the core white space EVERA is targeting.
  • The break-even volume is 4,270 units per month, equivalent to ~57% capacity utilisation at the startup's modeled scale.
  • The product's formulation (60% date seed powder, 40% chicory root powder) and kraft-paper packaging both reinforce the same circular-economy, waste-to-value brand narrative.
Key Takeaways
  • Valuable business opportunities often emerge at the intersection of consumer health needs, sustainability concerns, and creative resource utilization — exactly where EVERA sits.
  • EVERA's advantage is currently temporary rather than sustained (per VRIO) — product validation, brand story and distribution partnerships need to be built quickly before the caffeine-free niche attracts better-funded entrants.
  • A phased, capital-efficient go-to-market (e-commerce and DTC first, specialty retail second, HoReCa/institutional last) sequences cash-intensive physical retail expansion after brand validation rather than before it.
  • As a student-founder venture, EVERA's biggest near-term constraints are capital and production infrastructure, not product concept or market timing.
