1. Executive Summary
This report presents a comprehensive equity due diligence assessment of Britannia Industries  
Limited (BIL) based on the latest available market data as of 29 March 2026. The analysis  
incorporates the company's most recent quarterly results (Q3 FY26, ending December 2025), live  
stock  market  data,  analyst  estimates,  and  updated  strategic  developments  including  a  new  
Managing Director, a major management rejig, and the rollout of the Bel SA cheese joint venture.
Britannia Industries remains India's dominant biscuit manufacturer with a 34% market share in  
organised biscuits, a market capitalisation of approximately 1.32–1.36 lakh crore, and a current ₹  
share price of approximately 5,500 (NSE: BRITANNIA, as of 27 March 2026). Q3 FY26 results were ₹  
broadly positive: consolidated net profit rose 16.9% year-on-year to 680 crore, revenue grew 9.5% ₹  
YoY to 4,885 crore, and gross margins expanded a remarkable 450 basis points to 43.3%. ₹  
However, the stock trades at a demanding P/E of approximately 55–61x, which many analysts flag as  
stretched relative to single-digit to low double-digit earnings growth.
The company is navigating a pivotal leadership transition. Rakshit Hargave, a seasoned FMCG  
executive formerly of Birla Opus and Beiersdorf (NIVEA), assumed charge as MD & CEO effective 15  
December 2025. A broader C-suite rejig — including new appointments in Sales, Marketing, and  
Dairy  —  signals  Britannia's  intent  to  accelerate  execution  across  brand  building,  distribution  
expansion, and category diversification. Analyst consensus is moderately bullish, with 25 analysts  
recommending Buy against 2 Sell ratings, and an average 12-month price target of 6,766. ₹
Key  risks  include  stretched  valuations,  commodity  price  sensitivity,  intensifying  competition  
(particularly from ITC's Sunfeast), and execution uncertainty in new categories such as dairy and  
cheese. This report provides a detailed, data-driven view of the company's financial trajectory,  
competitive positioning, market sentiment, and investment outlook as of Q1 2026.

3. Company Overview & Governance
3.1 Corporate Profile
Britannia Industries Limited, founded in 1892 and incorporated as a public limited company in 1918,  
is one of India's oldest and largest fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) companies. Headquartered  
operationally in Bengaluru (Whitefield) and registered in Kolkata, it is the food-processing arm of the  
Wadia Group — one of India's oldest conglomerates, with interests spanning FMCG, real estate,  
textiles, aerospace, and chemicals.
The company's product scope spans bakery products (biscuits, bread, cakes, rusk, croissants,  
wafers) and dairy (milk, cheese, yoghurt, butter, flavoured beverages). It sells under over 15 brand  
names and exports to approximately 80 countries, making it a genuine Indian multinational consumer  
goods company.

3.2 Leadership Transition: Key Development
A critical recent development is the appointment of Rakshit Hargave as MD & CEO from 15  
December 2025, replacing long-serving executive N. Venkataraman. Hargave brings deep consumer  
brand experience — as former CEO of Birla Opus (paints), head of Beiersdorf's NIVEA business  
across  India,  Africa,  ASEAN  and  ANZ,  and  COO  at  Lakme  Lever  (HUL).  He  also  played  a  
foundational role in Domino's 30-minute delivery model through Jubilant FoodWorks.
Simultaneously, Britannia executed a broad C-suite rejig effective December 2025 – February 2026:  
Abhishek Sinha became Chief Sales Transformation Officer, Subhashis Basu was elevated to Chief  
Business Officer (Dairy), and Puneet Das was appointed Chief Marketing Officer. Brokerages see  
this reshuffle as a positive catalyst, particularly for brand building and distribution acceleration.
4. Financial Performance: FY25 to Q3 FY26
4.1 Full Year FY2025 Performance
For the full fiscal year FY2025 (April 2024 – March 2025), Britannia reported consolidated revenues  
of 17,943 crore, representing 7% year-on-year growth. Net profit for FY25 stood at 2,179 crore, up₹ ₹  
~2% YoY. The relatively muted profit growth compared to revenue growth reflected commodity cost  
pressures through the year. The board declared a total annual dividend of 100 per share for FY25, a ₹  
25% increase from FY24's 80, reflecting strong underlying cash generation. ₹
4.2 Latest Quarter: Q3 FY26 (Oct–Dec 2025) — Strong Rebound
Q3 FY26 marks Britannia's strongest quarterly performance in recent memory. Consolidated net  
profit rose 16.9% YoY to 680 crore (vs. 582 crore in Q3 FY25), exceeding analyst estimates. ₹ ₹  
Revenue reached an all-time quarterly high of 4,885–4,970 crore (up 9.5% YoY). EBITDA grew ₹  
15.6% YoY to 977 crore, with EBITDA margin expanding to 19.5% from 18.4% a year earlier. Gross ₹  
margins expanded a notable 450 basis points YoY to 43.3%, driven by stable commodity prices and  
efficient cost management.
October 2025 was impacted by GST-led trade disruptions (following rate rationalisation on biscuits),  
but demand fully normalised in November–December, with biscuit sales growing approximately 12%  
in those two months. Management attributed the recovery to sustained media investment, portfolio  
innovations including '50-50 Dipped' and 'Veg' cake variants, and the launch of 'Doodh' Marie Gold.

5.2 Active Competitive Developments
The competitive battle between Britannia and ITC has escalated into legal terrain. In early 2026, ITC  
formally accused Britannia of trade dress infringement in the packaging of its cheese-flavoured  
biscuit, claiming that the black-orange-yellow packaging of its Sunfeast Wowzers product constitutes  
an original copyright-protected artistic work. The case underlines how aggressively ITC is contesting  
Britannia's positioning in premium biscuit segments.
On pricing dynamics: following GST rate rationalisation on biscuits, some national brands remained  
at 4.5 and 9 price points rather than reverting to 5/ 10. Nomura estimates that price parity will₹ ₹ ₹ ₹  
normalise across players by Q4 FY26, which should accelerate volume growth to high single digits  
and reduce competitive distortion. Britannia's Low-Unit-Pack (LUP) portfolio, constituting 60–65% of  
revenues, is especially sensitive to these dynamics.
In dairy, the primary competitive pressure comes from Amul (GCMMF) and Nestlé India, who  
dominate cheese and value-added dairy. The Bel SA JV, backed by the French group's global  
cheese expertise, is designed to give Britannia a credible technological foundation in this category.

leadership) justify a premium to FMCG peers, the current P/E of 55–61x appears stretched given  
earnings growth in the low double digits.

7. Risk Analysis
7.1 Commodity & Input Cost Risk
Despite Q3 FY26 gross margin expansion, commodity risks remain material. Key inputs — wheat,  
palm oil, sugar, and cocoa — are globally traded and subject to weather, geopolitical, and supply-
chain shocks. The benign wheat environment ahead of the March 2026 harvest season is providing  
temporary relief, but any reversal could pressure margins rapidly. Cocoa prices surged ~70% YoY in  
2024–25, and though conditions have stabilised, structural supply deficits in West Africa remain a  
long-term concern for chocolate-based products.
7.2 Valuation & Multiple Contraction Risk
At 55–61x earnings, Britannia is priced for considerable growth expectations. If Q4 FY26 or FY27  
earnings disappoint — due to demand weakness, pricing pressure, or elevated spend on brand  
building — a de-rating is plausible. FII selling is already a visible trend, and should domestic mutual  
funds begin trimming (having accumulated sharply), supply-demand dynamics in the stock could turn  
unfavourable.
7.3 Competitive Intensity & Pricing Pressure
ITC's Sunfeast platform is well-funded and aggressive. The GST-related price disruption in Q1–Q2  
FY26 demonstrated the vulnerability of the biscuit category to competitive price behaviour. Should  
competitors choose to maintain lower price points at the expense of profitability, Britannia faces a  
choice between protecting market share (at the cost of margin) or protecting margin (at the risk of  
volume loss).
7.4 Management Transition Execution Risk
The arrival of a new MD & CEO (Rakshit Hargave) alongside a broad leadership rejig introduces  
transition risk. While brokerage consensus views the appointments positively, any strategic missteps  
or culture changes during the transition could create near-term operational disruptions. Investors will  
closely watch Hargave's first full year of results (FY27) for evidence of strategic continuity or pivot.
7.5 Dairy Category Execution Risk
The  cheese  and  dairy  ambition  is  well-articulated  but  operationally  unproven  at  scale.  The  
Maharashtra cheese plant (Bel SA JV, opened October 2024) is still in early commercial ramp-up.  
Competing  with  Amul's  cooperative  pricing  advantages  and  Nestlé's  global  R&D  in  dairy  is  
structurally challenging. Near-term margin contributions from dairy may be limited.
7.6 Regulatory Risk
India's FSSAI has been progressively tightening food safety, labelling, and health claim regulations.  
Any mandated reformulation, front-of-pack labelling requirements, or reclassification of product  
categories under GST could increase compliance costs or impact brand communication.
8. Strategic Outlook & Growth Drivers
8.1 New MD & Fresh Strategic Priorities
Rakshit Hargave's background in brand-led consumer companies (NIVEA, Domino's, Birla Opus)  
suggests Britannia's strategic focus under his leadership will centre on brand elevation, channel  
innovation, and category expansion — consistent with the company's articulated 'Total Foods  
Company' vision. His experience with quick-service restaurant supply chains (Jubilant FoodWorks)  
could also be valuable as Britannia expands its foodservice and out-of-home distribution.
8.2 Rural Market Penetration
Rural India represents the highest-growth opportunity for packaged food, and Britannia has been  
investing in direct distribution infrastructure. Rural FMCG growth has consistently outpaced urban  
markets, supported by government rural income schemes and rising aspirations. The Tiger brand  
(fortified, affordable) is the primary vehicle for deepening rural penetration. Nomura models high  
single-digit volume growth in FY27 as rural distribution matures.
8.3 GST Rate Rationalisation — Structural Tailwind
The GST rate rationalisation on biscuits, while disruptive in the short run (Q1–Q2 FY26), is expected  
to settle into a structural positive. Once price parity normalises across players (expected Q4 FY26),  
Britannia's Low-Unit-Pack portfolio — a key driver of entry-level consumption — should benefit from  
dual price-point reset, with both 5 and 10 packs offering higher grammage to consumers. ₹ ₹
8.4 Dairy & Cheese Category Ramp-Up
The Maharashtra cheese manufacturing plant, opened in October 2024 through the Bel SA joint  
venture, is being positioned as the cornerstone of Britannia's premium dairy ambitions. The facility  
combines Bel Group's French cheese-making technology with Britannia's distribution strength.  
Commercial ramp-up is expected through FY27, with cheese potentially contributing meaningfully to  
dairy revenues within 2–3 years. Subhashis Basu's appointment as Chief Business Officer (Dairy)  
signals dedicated leadership focus on this segment.
8.5 Premiumisation & Product Innovation
Launches such as '50-50 Dipped' (chocolate-coated premium variant), 'Veg' cake variants, 'Doodh'  
Marie Gold, and the Treat Croissant line are targeted at the growing urban consumer appetite for  
indulgent, differentiated snacks. The Good Day, Bourbon, and NutriChoice platforms each have  
strong premiumisation potential. Puneet Das as the new CMO is expected to reinvigorate consumer-
facing brand communication.
8.6 Digital & Operational Transformation
Britannia's partnership with Accenture for digital transformation — encompassing supply chain  
analytics, demand forecasting, and automation — is expected to reduce waste and improve margin  
visibility. AI-powered consumer engagement tools, including the NutriPlus health scoring app, reflect  
Britannia's  intent  to  build  direct-to-consumer  relationships  and  leverage  health  and  wellness  
positioning for the NutriChoice franchise.
9. ESG & Corporate Responsibility
9.1 Environmental
Britannia's  sustainability  vision  is  anchored  in  four  pillars:  minimising  environmental  footprint,  
responsible sourcing, food safety and quality, and community nutrition. The company is investing in  
renewable energy at manufacturing sites and advanced analytics to track and reduce emissions.  
Focused ESG spending aligns with its cost-optimisation agenda, ensuring that sustainability is not  
just ethical but commercially rational.
9.2 Social
Britannia has built a meaningful social presence through the NutriChoice NutriPlus app (personalised  
health tracking), the Marie Gold 'HerStore' digital marketplace for women entrepreneurs (over 2,500+  
products  and  services  listed),  and  nutrition  programs  targeting  malnutrition  in  marginalised  
communities. Kincentric Best Employer recognition for five consecutive years (2019–2024), with a  
Top 10 ranking among all Indian companies in 2024, reflects strong talent practices.
9.3 Governance
The Wadia Group's 50.6% promoter stake with zero pledging is a best-in-class governance indicator  
in the Indian listed company context. The board includes independent directors with strong academic  
and public-sector credentials. Britannia complies with all SEBI LODR (Listing Obligations and  
Disclosure Requirements) obligations. The absence of any significant related-party transaction  
controversies in recent years adds to governance credibility.

11. Conclusion & Due Diligence Assessment
Britannia Industries Limited enters Q4 FY26 with meaningful operational momentum. Q3 FY26  
delivered record quarterly revenues, a 450 basis point gross margin expansion, and 17% profit  
growth — figures that affirm the company's structural resilience despite a disruptive macro and  
competitive  environment.  The  incoming  leadership  under  Rakshit  Hargave  has  been  greeted  
positively by the market and brokerage community, who view the C-suite refresh as a catalyst for  
faster execution across brand building, distribution, and category diversification.
From a market perspective, however, the current price of ~ 5,500 and P/E of ~58x presents a ₹  
nuanced picture. Analyst consensus is constructive (25 Buy vs. 2 Sell), with Nomura and Motilal  
Oswal setting targets of 7,150–7,275, implying 30–32% upside. But the technical setup is mixed: ₹  
the stock is below its 50-day and 200-day moving averages, FIIs have reduced their stake by ~158  
basis points over five quarters, and the MarketsMOJO downgrade to Sell in late March 2026 reflects  
near-term caution. Any meaningful re-rating likely requires sustained double-digit earnings growth  
across 2–3 consecutive quarters.
For long-term strategic investors, Britannia's fundamentals remain compelling: sector leadership,  
capital-light business model, exceptional ROCE, zero debt-stress, consistent dividends, and a  
professional management team now with fresh FMCG talent at the helm. The dairy opportunity, while  
nascent, represents a credible adjacency, and the GST tailwind could accelerate volume growth  
through FY27.
The primary due diligence concern is valuation — not business quality. Investors entering at current  
prices  are  pricing  in  a  significant  improvement  in  growth  trajectory  that  has  yet  to  be  fully  
demonstrated under the new leadership. A position build-up strategy tied to quarterly earnings  
milestones or a price correction to the 4,800–5,000 range (where P/E moderates to ~48–50x) would ₹  
present a more attractive risk-reward profile.

1. Introduction
The contemporary labor market is characterized by rapid structural changes driven by technological advancement, digital transformation, globalization, and evolving organizational requirements. As industries increasingly adopt data-driven decision-making, the importance of understanding workforce behavior, compensation expectations, and professional mobility has grown substantially. For organizations, recruiters, and policymakers, the ability to analyze and interpret large-scale employment data has become a strategic necessity rather than an operational luxury.
Salary expectations represent one of the most critical dimensions of job seeker behavior. These expectations are influenced by a complex interplay of factors such as professional experience, educational attainment, skill composition, industry exposure, geographic location, and current compensation levels. Inaccurate assessment of these expectations can result in inefficient recruitment processes, misaligned compensation structures, and increased attrition rates. Consequently, there is a growing need to employ analytical techniques that can systematically evaluate these factors and uncover meaningful patterns within employment data.
Business Analytics provides a structured framework for extracting insights from large and complex datasets. Through the application of statistical analysis, data visualization, clustering techniques, and predictive modelling, Business Analytics enables organizations to move beyond intuition-based decision-making toward evidence-based workforce strategies. In the context of human resource analytics, such techniques facilitate a deeper understanding of labor market dynamics, candidate segmentation, and compensation determinants.
This report presents an extensive Business Analytics study based on a large-scale Job Seekers dataset containing 913,384 observations. The dataset encompasses a wide range of attributes related to job seekers, including expected salary, current salary, years of work experience, highest level of education, area of specialization, key skills, industry affiliation, city of residence, and preferred job location. The breadth and depth of this dataset provide a robust foundation for analyzing employment trends across multiple dimensions.
The analysis is structured around three core analytical components. First, Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is conducted to examine data quality, distributions, central tendencies, outliers, and preliminary relationships among variables. EDA serves as a critical foundation for identifying patterns and informing subsequent modeling decisions. Second, Clustering Analysis is employed to segment job seekers into homogeneous groups based on shared characteristics, thereby enabling a deeper understanding of distinct candidate profiles. Third, Regression Analysis is applied to evaluate the extent to which experience, education, and skills explain variations in expected salary, and to assess the predictive strength of these variables.
The primary objective of this study is to derive meaningful, data-driven insights that contribute to a better understanding of salary expectations and job seeker behavior. By integrating descriptive, diagnostic, and predictive analytics, the report aims to demonstrate the practical application of Business Analytics concepts in a real-world employment context. The findings of this analysis are expected to be relevant not only for academic evaluation but also for organizations seeking to improve recruitment effectiveness, compensation planning, and workforce strategy.


2. Questions Selected
The selection of analytical questions forms the foundation of any data-driven study, as it determines the direction, scope, and relevance of the analysis. In the context of this Business Analytics project, the questions have been carefully formulated to address both descriptive and analytical aspects of job seeker behaviour. Given the scale and richness of the dataset, the selected questions aim to capture salary dynamics, professional characteristics, and labour market trends in a structured and systematic manner.
The first question focuses on understanding the demographic and professional composition of job seekers represented in the dataset. This includes examining variables such as work experience, education level, specialization, skills, industry affiliation, and geographic distribution. Analysing these characteristics provides essential context and enables a holistic understanding of the workforce profile under consideration. Without this foundational understanding, subsequent analytical findings would lack interpretative depth.
The second question examines how salary expectations are distributed across different dimensions, including experience levels, education categories, skills, and cities. Salary expectations are not uniform and often exhibit significant variation due to individual and market-specific factors. By analyzing salary distributions and identifying patterns of skewness or dispersion, the study aims to highlight inequality in compensation expectations and assess how these expectations differ across various subgroups within the workforce.
The third question investigates the relationship between expected salary and key explanatory variables, such as work experience, current salary, education, and skills. Understanding these relationships is essential for identifying which factors have meaningful influence on salary expectations. This question is addressed through correlation analysis and forms the basis for subsequent predictive modelling.
The fourth question explores whether job seekers can be meaningfully segmented into distinct groups based on their professional and compensation-related attributes. Clustering analysis is employed to determine whether natural groupings exist within the data and to identify common characteristics within each segment. This segmentation is particularly useful for recruiters and organizations seeking to tailor hiring strategies to different candidate profiles.
The fifth question evaluates the predictive capability of experience, education, and skills with respect to expected salary. Using regression analysis, the study assesses the extent to which these variables explain variation in salary expectations and quantifies their individual contributions. This question is central to understanding the limitations of traditional predictors and the need for more nuanced modeling approaches.
Finally, the study examines behavioral patterns related to job mobility and location preference, particularly the relationship between current city and preferred job location. This question provides insight into workforce mobility and geographic flexibility, which are critical considerations in modern labor markets.
Collectively, these questions ensure that the analysis remains comprehensive, logically structured, and aligned with the objectives of Business Analytics. They enable the integration of descriptive insights with advanced analytical techniques, ultimately supporting informed conclusions and actionable recommendations.

3. Problem Statement
The modern labor market is characterized by increasing complexity, driven by rapid technological change, evolving skill requirements, and heightened workforce mobility. Organizations today operate in an environment where competition for talent is intense, and effective recruitment and compensation strategies are critical for organizational performance. Despite the availability of large volumes of employment-related data, many organizations struggle to translate this data into meaningful insights that can support strategic decision-making.
One of the most significant challenges faced by employers and recruiters is accurately understanding and managing salary expectations of job seekers. Salary expectations are influenced by multiple interdependent factors, including work experience, educational background, skill composition, industry exposure, geographic location, and current compensation levels. However, traditional approaches to salary benchmarking often rely on limited datasets, broad averages, or subjective judgment, which may fail to capture the nuanced relationships present in large and diverse labor markets.
From a job seeker perspective, misalignment between expected salary and market realities can lead to prolonged job search durations, dissatisfaction, and reduced employment outcomes. From an organizational perspective, inaccurate assessment of salary expectations can result in inefficient hiring processes, increased recruitment costs, and higher attrition rates. These challenges highlight the need for a systematic, data-driven approach to analyzing job seeker profiles and compensation expectations.
The primary problem addressed in this study is the lack of empirical understanding of the key determinants of expected salary and the absence of effective segmentation of job seekers based on observable characteristics. While variables such as experience, education, and skills are commonly assumed to be strong predictors of salary expectations, their actual explanatory and predictive power remain unclear when analyzed at scale. Furthermore, it is not evident whether job seekers naturally form distinct groups based on these attributes or whether salary expectations are shaped by more complex and unobserved factors.
Therefore, this study seeks to address the following core problem:
How can Business Analytics techniques be applied to a large-scale job seeker dataset to identify the factors influencing expected salary, segment job seekers into meaningful clusters, and evaluate the predictive effectiveness of experience, education, and skills?
By addressing this problem through Exploratory Data Analysis, clustering, and regression modeling, the study aims to bridge the gap between raw employment data and actionable workforce insights. The outcomes of this analysis are expected to support more informed recruitment strategies, improved compensation planning, and a deeper understanding of labor market behavior in a data-driven context.





4. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Objective & Dataset Description
4.1 Objective of Exploratory Data Analysis
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) constitutes a critical initial phase in any data-driven study, particularly when working with large and complex datasets. The primary objective of EDA is to develop an in-depth understanding of the underlying data structure before applying advanced analytical and predictive techniques. In the context of this study, EDA serves as a foundational step to ensure data reliability, uncover hidden patterns, and inform subsequent clustering and regression analyses.
One of the key objectives of EDA is to assess data quality. Large real-world datasets often contain missing values, inconsistencies, outliers, and formatting issues that can significantly distort analytical results if left unaddressed. Through EDA, the dataset is systematically examined to identify the extent of missing data, validate variable formats, and evaluate the consistency of observations across different attributes. This process ensures that the data used for analysis is both accurate and meaningful.
Another important objective of EDA is to understand the distribution of key variables, particularly those related to salary and experience. Salary data in labor market studies is often characterized by skewness and extreme values, reflecting inequality and heterogeneity across job roles and experience levels. By analyzing measures of central tendency, dispersion, and distribution shape, EDA enables the identification of such characteristics and informs decisions regarding data transformation and modeling techniques.
EDA also aims to explore relationships among variables. Preliminary correlation analysis provides insight into which factors are potentially associated with expected salary and which variables may have limited explanatory power. These insights are essential for hypothesis formulation and for selecting appropriate variables for clustering and regression models.
Finally, EDA supports the identification of outliers and anomalous observations. In the context of job seeker data, unusually high salary expectations may represent senior professionals, niche roles, or aspirational reporting. Rather than indiscriminately removing these observations, EDA helps determine how they should be treated to preserve valuable information while maintaining analytical robustness.
Overall, the objective of EDA in this study is to transform raw job seeker data into a structured, interpretable, and analytically sound foundation that supports deeper investigation through clustering and regression analysis.
4.2 Dataset Description
The dataset used in this study comprises 913,384 individual job seeker records, making it a large-scale and highly representative sample of the employment market. The dataset contains 18 original variables, capturing a wide range of demographic, professional, and compensation-related attributes. Such scale and diversity enable comprehensive analysis of labor market trends across multiple dimensions.
Key variables included in the dataset are:
Expected Salary: The compensation level anticipated by the job seeker.
Current Salary: The individual’s existing compensation, where available.
Work Experience (Years): Total professional experience expressed in years.
Highest Education Level: The highest academic qualification attained by the job seeker.
Area of Specialization: Domain or functional specialization.
Key Skills: Technical and functional skills reported by the individual.
Industry: Industry sector in which the job seeker is employed or seeking employment.
City: Current city of residence.
